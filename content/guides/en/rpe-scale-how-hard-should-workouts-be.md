Ask any coach about the RPE scale, how hard should a workout be, and you will get a number instead of a speech: most of your sessions should land at 7 or 8 out of 10. At home there is nobody watching your last rep and saying "stop" or "add two", so that number has to come from you. RPE, the rate of perceived exertion, is the tool that makes it possible: after a session you rate the effort from 1 to 10, and that rating, together with how much of the plan you actually completed, decides the volume of the next one. The method goes back to the Swedish physiologist Gunnar Borg; strength coaches later tied it to reps in reserve. Below: how to read the numbers, which effort belongs to which day, and the exact rules Forma uses to recalculate your workouts.

## What you will get from this guide

- The 1–10 RPE table with the same descriptors the app shows, translated into reps in reserve.
- Target zones: how hard a technique day, a main strength day and a test day should feel.

## The RPE scale: how hard should a workout be, in numbers

RPE stands for rate of perceived exertion. Forma uses the Borg CR10 version: 1 to 10, where 10 means you could not have done one more rep. The key idea: you are rating how close to failure the work was, not how tired you feel after a long day.

| RPE | How it feels | Reps in reserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | Very easy, easy | Plenty; this is warm-up territory |
| 3–4 | Comfortable, moderate | Plenty |
| 5 | Working | 5 or more |
| 6 | Challenging | About 4 |
| 7 | Hard | 3 |
| 8 | Very hard | 2 |
| 9 | Near max | 1 |
| 10 | Maximal | 0 |

### What reps in reserve means

Reps in reserve, RIR, is how many clean reps you could still have done at the moment you stopped. Zourdos and colleagues (2016) showed that trained lifters judge the last one to three reps before failure quite accurately, and that is how RPE in CrossFit and strength training maps onto numbers: 7 means three reps left, 8 means two, 9 means one, 10 means none. Beginners usually underestimate: "I cannot do another one" often means four or five are still there. That is normal; accuracy comes with practice.

A simple calibration: do a set of [air squats](exercise:air_squat) to what feels like RPE 8, rest a minute, then do a second set to honest technical failure. If the second set had five more reps than the first, your "8" is really a 6.

## How hard a home workout should be, day by day

Not every session should be hard. Autoregulation training is exactly the skill of giving different days different efforts.

- **Technique days and the first two weeks of a course: RPE 5–6.** You are learning the movement, and the reps in reserve are what keep the form intact. [Knee push-ups](exercise:knee_push_up) at RPE 6 do more for you than full push-ups at RPE 10 with a sagging lower back.
- **Main strength and interval days: RPE 7–8.** This is where strength and conditioning grow while you still recover between sessions. The ACSM position stand on progression recommends adding 2–10 percent of load once you comfortably exceed the target reps, not grinding to failure every time.
- **Tests and benchmarks: RPE 9–10.** Every few weeks, on their own day, on fresh legs. Max [burpees](exercise:burpee) in a minute is a test, not a weekly workout.

The WHO recommends that adults get 150–300 minutes of moderate activity a week, or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity. Moderate is roughly RPE 4–6, and it should make up most of your week. If you have high blood pressure, a heart condition or have not trained in a long time, talk to a doctor before working at RPE 9–10.

## How Forma turns your rating into the next workout

After every session the app asks two things: effort from 1 to 10 with the descriptors from the table above, and how you feel, with a separate option for "something hurt". The second number the app computes itself: **completion**, the share of the planned work you actually did, weighted by how long each exercise takes.

The rules are simple and always apply in this order:

| What happened | Volume change |
| --- | --- |
| You reported pain | −10 percent, plus a note to reduce the load and see a professional if it repeats |
| RPE 9–10, or less than 80 percent completed | −5 percent |
| 95 percent or more completed at RPE 6 or lower | +5 percent |
| RPE 7–8 with at least 90 percent completed | +2 percent |
| Anything else | no change |

The percentages are of the course's authored volume. Every user has a volume multiplier: after the intake test it starts somewhere between 0.6 and 1.3, and from there it moves within 0.5–1.5. A 2–5 percent step per session sits at the low end of the ACSM "2–10 percent" rule on purpose: nobody is checking your form at home, so the steps stay small.

### The pre-workout recommendation

Before you start, Forma suggests one of three options and tells you why. "Harder" appears when your last two sessions were rated 6 or lower with at least 95 percent completed and at least 48 hours have passed since the last one; those are the 48–72 hours of recovery between sessions for the same muscles that the ACSM recommends for novices. "Easier" appears when the last session involved pain, was rated 9 or 10, was less than 80 percent completed, happened less than 24 hours ago, or you logged 15,000 steps or more yesterday. Otherwise it is "as planned". The choice stays yours: "easier" trims volume by 15 percent and lengthens rest by 15 percent, "harder" adds 15 percent volume and cuts rest by 10 percent. The harder option pays more points, but easier is never punished: you still get 80 percent of the points for a fully completed session. Deload weeks inside the courses work separately: volume ×0.65 and rest ×1.2.

## A sample workout with RPE targets

A level-2 bodyweight session, about 30 minutes. Warm up first for five minutes: [jogging in place](exercise:jog_in_place), [arm circles](exercise:arm_circles), ten [squat-to-stands](exercise:squat_to_stand).

**Strength block, 3 sets.** 15 air squats, 10 [push-ups](exercise:push_up), 12 [reverse lunges](exercise:reverse_lunge), 30 seconds of [plank](exercise:plank). 20 seconds between exercises, 75 between sets. Target RPE 7: in the last set of push-ups you should have three reps left.

**Finisher, AMRAP 6 minutes.** 4 burpees, 8 [sit-ups](exercise:sit_up). Target RPE 8 by the end of minute six, not sooner.

How the app scales it. Say your test set the multiplier at 0.8: you get 12 squats instead of 15, 8 push-ups instead of 10, and if you are at level 1 and pick "easier", burpees become [half burpees](exercise:half_burpee). You finish everything and rate it a 5: the multiplier becomes 0.85 and next time you squat 13. You finish everything and rate it a 7: the multiplier becomes 0.82, which may not change a single number in this session yet, but two such ratings in a row take it to 0.84 and the squats to 13. You rate it a 9, or quit the finisher in minute three: 0.75, and the volume drops slightly. No single rating changes the workout sharply; ten honest ratings in a row put the volume exactly where you need it.

## Common mistakes

- **Rating the day, not the work.** Poor sleep and stress matter, but RPE is about proximity to failure in the sets.
- **Giving a polite 7.** How to know if a workout is too easy: you did everything and could repeat the session right now. That is a 5, and the app needs to hear it or the volume will not grow.
- **Chasing 9–10 every session.** Two or three of those in a row and the next week goes to recovery. Progress comes from volume at 7–8, not from failure.
- **Confusing pain with effort.** Burning muscles are effort. Sharp joint pain is a stop sign, and in the app it is the "something hurt" option, which lowers the load.
- **Ignoring "easier" after a short gap.** That recommendation after a session less than a day ago is about recovery, not weakness. How many sessions a week make sense is covered in the [training frequency guide](guide:training-frequency).

## How to progress

1. **Weeks 1–2.** Rate every session and change nothing by hand. The job is learning to tell a 5 from a 7.
2. **Weeks 3–4.** Once a week, accept "harder" when it appears. Notice how +15 percent volume with −10 percent rest feels.
3. **Month 2.** Run the calibration set to failure once every two weeks to check that your "8" has not drifted.
4. **The sign the system works:** most sessions at RPE 7–8, two or three a month at 5–6, and a 9–10 test every few weeks with rising numbers.

## Wrap-up

RPE is the simplest honest tool for setting the load at home: one number after the workout plus the share of the plan you completed. Hard days at 7–8, easy days at 5–6, tests at 9–10, and the volume grows in 2–5 percent steps at exactly the pace you recover.
