Kettlebell workouts at home for beginners are the fastest route from "I squat and do push-ups" to real strength training. One bell takes up half a metre of floor, needs no rack or bench, and every movement with it loads the legs, back, shoulders and grip at the same time. The catch is the learning curve. The swing, the clean and the snatch are easy to do badly, and a bad swing gives you a sore lower back instead of a strong one. This is a coach's plan for your first two months: which bell to buy, the five movements to learn first and in what order, how to build a week out of them, and when you have earned the harder lifts.

## What you will get from this guide

- How to pick the weight and type of your first kettlebell.
- Five foundational movements in the right order, with the technique points that matter.

## Kettlebell workouts at home for beginners: which bell to buy

You need one kettlebell, not a set. Choose the weight for the swing, not the press: the swing is driven by the hips, and a bell that is too light teaches you to lift it with your arms. My starting points for people with no strength-training background: women 8 kg (about 18 lb), or 12 kg if you are already fit; men 12 kg (about 26 lb), or 16 kg with some barbell experience. Pressing that bell will be hard at first and there are substitutes for that, but the deadlift, the goblet squat and the swing will feel right from day one.

What to look for:

| Feature | Pick | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Type | One-piece cast iron | Durable, no welded seams on the handle, sold everywhere |
| Handle | Smooth, unpainted, wide enough for two hands | Seams and paint tear skin on swings; a narrow handle blocks the two-hand grip |
| Coating | No vinyl or rubber on the handle | Coatings stick to the palm and stop the bell rotating in your hand |
| Base | Flat | The bell stands still between sets and does not rock |

Beyond the bell you need a mat and about a metre and a half of clear floor in front of you: a swing brings the bell to chest height, and a coffee table in that zone is a problem.

## Five movements to learn first

The order matters. Each movement builds on the one before it, and skipping a step almost always shows up later as pain or a technique that will not improve.

### Kettlebell deadlift

The [kettlebell deadlift](exercise:kb_deadlift) teaches the one thing everything else depends on: hinging at the hips with a flat back. The bell sits between your feet, you push the hips back, grab the handle with both hands and stand up, extending knees and hips together. The back stays flat at the bottom and at the top. Do not move on to the swing until the deadlift is automatic.

### Goblet squat

The [kettlebell goblet squat](exercise:kb_goblet_squat) holds the bell at the chest by the horns, elbows tucked, and squats below parallel. The weight in front keeps your torso upright and opens the hips, which is why many people squat deeper and cleaner with a bell than without one. This is your main leg exercise for the first month.

### Kettlebell swing

The [kettlebell swing](exercise:kb_swing) is the deadlift in motion. The bell swings back between your legs, you snap the hips forward, and the bell floats up to chest height on its own. The arms are ropes; they do not lift the bell. At the top the body is a straight line with the glutes tight; at the bottom the bell passes above the knees rather than dropping toward the floor. Exhale sharply on every swing.

### Kettlebell press

The [kettlebell press](exercise:kb_press) is your first overhead movement. The bell rests on the forearm with the elbow close to the ribs; you press to full lockout and lower under control. If your bell is too heavy to press, do [pike push-ups](exercise:pike_push_up) for now: they prepare the shoulders for vertical pressing.

### Carries

The [farmer carry](exercise:farmer_carry) with a bell in each hand, or the [suitcase carry](exercise:kb_suitcase_carry) with one, look easy but build grip and a stable trunk like nothing else. Walk slowly, shoulders down, torso not leaning toward the bell. Start with 30 to 40 seconds per side.

One more: the [kettlebell halo](exercise:kb_halo), eight slow circles around the head with a light bell, is the best shoulder warm-up before any of these sessions.

## How to build your week

Three kettlebell sessions a week with a rest day between them is the right starting frequency. More is not better: swings and deadlifts load the hamstrings and lower back heavily, and they do not recover overnight. On rest days, walk. The WHO recommends 150 to 300 minutes of moderate activity a week, and walking covers most of that.

| Day | Focus | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monday | Squat and press | Goblet squats, presses, carries, core |
| Wednesday | Swing school | Deadlifts, swings, halos, carries |
| Friday | Metabolic | EMOM or a short AMRAP of swings, squats and burpees |

That is exactly how the [Kettlebell Power course](course:kettlebell) is laid out: a strength day, a swing-technique day and a short interval day. EMOM and AMRAP are explained in the [guide to workout formats](guide:workout-formats).

## A sample kettlebell workout at home

A 30 to 35 minute session for the first month. One bell, medium weight.

**Warm-up, two rounds.** 20 [jumping jacks](exercise:jumping_jack), 8 kettlebell halos, 6 [squat-to-stands](exercise:squat_to_stand), 10 [leg swings](exercise:leg_swing) per side.

**Strength, three sets.** 10 kettlebell deadlifts, 8 goblet squats, 5 presses per arm. Rest 20 seconds between exercises and 75 seconds between sets. The tempo is slow: two seconds down, one second up.

**EMOM 9 minutes.** Minute 1: 12 kettlebell swings. Minute 2: 8 goblet squats. Minute 3: 6 [half burpees](exercise:half_burpee). Three rounds. The swings should take no more than 20 to 25 seconds; if they take longer, you are lifting with your arms or the bell is too heavy for the pace.

**Core and cool-down.** Two sets of a 40-second [plank](exercise:plank), then 40 seconds per side of the [hamstring stretch](exercise:hamstring_stretch) and the [hip flexor stretch](exercise:hip_flexor_stretch), and 45 seconds in [child's pose](exercise:child_pose).

## Common mistakes

- **Squatting the swing.** Knees bend, the bell drops toward the floor and the quads do the work instead of the hips. The swing is a hinge, not a squat: hips back, shins nearly vertical.
- **Lifting with the arms.** If your shoulders tire before your glutes, you are pulling. Snap the hips harder and let the arms relax.
- **Leaning back at the top.** The bell floats on and the hips push forward into the lower back. Squeeze the glutes and brace the abs at the top; the body is a plank.
- **A white-knuckle grip.** Hook the handle with your fingers rather than crushing it in your fist. Otherwise you get calluses and dead forearms within two weeks.
- **Pressing the swing bell too early.** A bell sized for swings is often too heavy to press, and that is normal. Do pike push-ups and press a lighter bell when you have one.
- **Swinging every day.** Hamstrings and lower back need recovery. Three days a week is the ceiling to start with.

If you have a disc injury, chronic lower-back pain, high blood pressure or have not trained in a long time, talk to a doctor before starting swings and overhead work.

## How to progress

1. **Weeks 1 and 2.** Deadlifts, goblet squats, carries and swings in short sets of 10. Learn to feel the hips rather than the bell.
2. **Weeks 3 and 4.** Swings in sets of 15 to 20, an EMOM of swings and squats, and your first [kettlebell clean](exercise:kb_clean). The clean is a swing that you stop at the shoulder.
3. **Month 2.** The press after the clean, the [Turkish get-up](exercise:kb_turkish_get_up) with a light bell as a separate skill, and a long 15-minute AMRAP. Increase volume by 5 to 10 percent at a time.
4. **The benchmark.** 100 swings and 50 goblet squats for time with a 12-minute cap. Write down the result; that is the number to beat in your next cycle.
5. **The second bell.** When 20 consecutive swings with your current bell feel easy and you can press it for 8 clean reps, buy the next size up, usually 4 kg heavier. That is also when the [kettlebell snatch](exercise:kb_snatch) opens up.

## Wrap-up

A kettlebell builds strength, conditioning and a resilient back, provided you learn it in the right order: deadlift, squat, swing, press, carries, and only then the clean and the snatch. Three sessions a week and a single bell are enough for the first two months.
