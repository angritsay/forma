Negative pull-up

Negative pull-ups are the lowering half only: you jump or climb to the top position and lower yourself slowly into a hang. Muscles are stronger while lengthening than while shortening, so even with zero pull-ups you can load your back and biceps through the full range. It is the shortest road to your first strict pull-up. Take at least three seconds to lower — faster than that and there is little benefit.

How to do it

  1. 1

    Use a chair or a jump to get into the top position: chin over the bar, overhand grip slightly wider than your shoulders.

  2. 2

    Set your shoulder blades, brace your abs and take your feet off the support so your arms carry the weight.

  3. 3

    Lower yourself over 3–5 seconds, resisting with the whole back, until your arms are fully straight.

  4. 4

    Step off or drop down, get back to the top and repeat.

Coaching cues

  • ✓ Count to three on the way down
  • ✓ Shoulder blades together
  • ✓ Brake all the way to the bottom

Common mistakes

  • ✕ Dropping instead of lowering
  • ✕ Letting go halfway before reaching a full hang
  • ✕ Shoulders shrugged up to the ears

Breathing

Inhale at the top, long exhale as you lower.

FAQ

What are the most common mistakes in Negative pull-up? +

The most common ones: dropping instead of lowering; letting go halfway before reaching a full hang; shoulders shrugged up to the ears. Watch for them from the first rep.

What should I focus on during Negative pull-up? +

Keep these cues in mind: count to three on the way down; shoulder blades together; brake all the way to the bottom.

How should I breathe during Negative pull-up? +

Inhale at the top, long exhale as you lower.
