Core: flexion

Hollow hold

The hollow hold is a gymnastics position: lower back pressed into the floor, arms and legs extended and hovering above it. It is the best way to learn to lock the abs and stop the spine from arching — the exact body position you need in push-ups, pull-ups and anything overhead. Difficulty is easy to dial: the closer your arms and legs are to the floor, the harder it gets.

How to do it

  1. 1

    Lie on your back, legs together, arms by your sides. Press your lower back into the floor so a hand could not slide underneath.

  2. 2

    Lift your shoulders and shoulder blades off the floor, chin slightly tucked, arms extended by your sides or overhead.

  3. 3

    Raise your straight legs 20–30 cm off the floor, toes pointed. Your body forms a shallow boat shape with the lower back still glued down.

  4. 4

    Hold for the prescribed time. If the lower back lifts, raise your legs higher or bend your knees — never let the back arch.

Coaching cues

  • ✓ Lower back into the floor
  • ✓ Ribs down
  • ✓ Point the toes

Common mistakes

  • ✕ Lower back lifts off the floor into an arch
  • ✕ Chin jutting up and the neck straining
  • ✕ Legs held too low for your current strength

Breathing

Short, even breaths in and out; do not hold your breath.

FAQ

What are the most common mistakes in Hollow hold? +

The most common ones: lower back lifts off the floor into an arch; chin jutting up and the neck straining; legs held too low for your current strength. Watch for them from the first rep.

What should I focus on during Hollow hold? +

Keep these cues in mind: lower back into the floor; ribs down; point the toes.

How should I breathe during Hollow hold? +

Short, even breaths in and out; do not hold your breath.
