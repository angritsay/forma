Core: anti-extension

Dead bug

The dead bug is the safest core exercise we use: lying on your back, you lower the opposite arm and leg while keeping your lower back on the floor. It teaches you to stabilise the trunk while the limbs move — which is what the abs actually do in real life and in sport. It suits a sensitive back and serves as the easy substitute for almost every other abs exercise. Reps are counted as the total for both sides.

How to do it

  1. 1

    Lie on your back, arms pointing straight up, hips and knees bent to 90 degrees — knees over hips, shins parallel to the floor.

  2. 2

    Press your lower back into the floor and brace: ribs down, no gap between the spine and the floor.

  3. 3

    Slowly lower your right arm overhead while extending your left leg forward until the heel is just above the floor.

  4. 4

    Return to the start and repeat with the other arm and leg — that is the next rep.

Coaching cues

  • ✓ Lower back into the floor
  • ✓ Slow and controlled
  • ✓ Opposite arm and leg

Common mistakes

  • ✕ Lower back lifting as the leg extends
  • ✕ Moving too fast and too loose
  • ✕ Holding your breath

Breathing

Exhale as the arm and leg move away, inhale as they return.

FAQ

What are the most common mistakes in Dead bug? +

The most common ones: lower back lifting as the leg extends; moving too fast and too loose; holding your breath. Watch for them from the first rep.

What should I focus on during Dead bug? +

Keep these cues in mind: lower back into the floor; slow and controlled; opposite arm and leg.

How should I breathe during Dead bug? +

Exhale as the arm and leg move away, inhale as they return.
