Turkish get-up

The Turkish get-up takes you from lying on the floor to standing with a kettlebell locked out overhead, and just as slowly back down. One rep takes about half a minute and passes through seven positions: elbow, hand, bridge, knee, lunge, standing — and back. It trains shoulder stability, core, hip mobility and focus all at once; no other movement teaches body control under load so well. Start with no weight or a water bottle to learn the sequence. Reps are per side.

How to do it

  1. 1

    Lie on your back with the bell in your right hand locked out over the shoulder, right knee bent with the foot flat. Left arm and leg rest on the floor about 45 degrees from the body.

  2. 2

    Push through the right foot and roll up onto your left elbow, then onto your left hand; eyes on the bell, the loaded arm vertical the whole time.

  3. 3

    Lift your hips into a bridge, sweep the left leg back under you and come onto the left knee; straighten the torso — you are in a lunge with the bell overhead.

  4. 4

    Stand up from the lunge. Then reverse every position back down to lying — that is one rep. Switch sides.

Coaching cues

  • ✓ Loaded arm stays vertical
  • ✓ Eyes on the bell
  • ✓ Pause in every position
  • ✓ Shoulder packed down

Common mistakes

  • ✕ Loaded arm bending or drifting sideways
  • ✕ Rushing and skipping positions
  • ✕ Bell too heavy for clean technique

Breathing

Calm breathing, exhale on each transition between positions.

FAQ

What are the most common mistakes in Turkish get-up? +

The most common ones: loaded arm bending or drifting sideways; rushing and skipping positions; bell too heavy for clean technique. Watch for them from the first rep.

What should I focus on during Turkish get-up? +

Keep these cues in mind: loaded arm stays vertical; eyes on the bell; pause in every position; shoulder packed down.

How should I breathe during Turkish get-up? +

Calm breathing, exhale on each transition between positions.
