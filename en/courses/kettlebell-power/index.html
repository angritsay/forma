About the course

A kettlebell is the most honest piece of home equipment: one lump of iron, and you can get any kind of load out of it. Every week of the course has three different days. "Squat & press": goblet squats, standing press, front-rack lunges and push-ups. "Swing school", where the main thread of the course lives — the deadlift, then the swing, the clean out of the swing, the press out of the clean, and in week five the snatch; every one of these days also has a Turkish get-up block and carries. And "Metabolic": swing EMOMs, a 10–25–10 ladder for time and a long AMRAP.

You start with a bodyweight test — push-ups in two minutes, squats in one, a max plank hold and burpees in a minute. The app uses it to set your starting volume, then fine-tunes it after every session from your effort rating. You never have to calculate the bell weight yourself: the program uses "light", "medium" and "heavy" labels and the app maps them to the bells you own. Week four is a deload: volume drops by about a third, the technique work stays. Week six holds the last strength day, the "100 swings + 50 goblet squats" benchmark for time and the retest.

All you need is one kettlebell and a mat. A weight guideline: men with some training experience — 16 kg, without it — 12 kg; women — 8–12 kg. If you own several bells, the app picks a lighter one for the press and the snatch and a heavier one for deadlifts and carries. You do not need a second bell: the farmer carry in this course is done with one, switching hands.

Who it is for

  • You have trained before: you can do 8–10 full push-ups in a row and hold a plank for a minute.
  • You own a kettlebell (or are ready to buy one) and want to be able to do everything with it, not just swing.
  • You want strength and conditioning at the same time — in 30–35 minutes, three times a week.
  • You finished Start or Bodyweight Engine and want to add iron to bodyweight.

What you will get

  • A clean kettlebell swing: hinge from the hips, flat back, the bell driven by the hips rather than the arms.
  • You learn the clean, the standing press, the snatch and the Turkish get-up — the whole basic kettlebell toolkit.
  • You complete the "100 swings + 50 goblet squats" benchmark and record a time to beat next cycle.
  • More push-ups, squats, plank time and burpees in the retest — you compare week one with week six.
  • A strong grip and a stable core from carries, get-ups and planks.

Sample workout

The first workout of the course, as in the app, before it is scaled to your level.

Squat & press A

Goblet squat and press: the technique

Three sets of the base combo: goblet squat, standing kettlebell press, front-rack reverse lunges and push-ups. Steady tempo, full range on every rep: in the squat the elbows go inside the knees, in the press the bell travels straight up past your face. A short core block to finish.

  1. Warm-up Circuit · 2 rounds

    Two easy rounds. Use a light bell in the warm-up: the goal is to wake up the shoulders, hips and back, not to get tired before the work starts.

    • Jog in place 45 sec
    • Kettlebell halo 8 reps · light
    • Squat to stand 6 reps
    • Arm circles 30 sec
  2. Strength Sets · 3 sets

    Three sets. Two seconds down, one second up. Twenty seconds between exercises, 75 between sets.

    • Kettlebell goblet squat 10 reps · medium
    • Kettlebell press 6 reps per side · medium
    • Kettlebell front-rack reverse lunge 12 reps · light
    • Push-up 10 reps
  3. Core Circuit · 2 rounds
    • Plank 40 sec
    • Dead bug 12 reps
  4. Cool-down Sets · 1 sets

    Put the bell down and breathe slowly. Stretch without bouncing until your heart rate is back to a talking pace.

    • Hip flexor stretch 40 sec per side
    • Hamstring stretch 40 sec per side
    • Child's pose 45 sec

How the app adapts

Before every workout you choose Easier, As usual or Harder, and the app suggests which. Afterwards you rate the effort from 1 to 10 and the next load shifts: +5 % when it was easy, −5 % when it was too hard. Deload weeks are built into the program.

Course FAQ

What kettlebell do I need? One or two?

One is enough — the whole course is built around a single bell, and the farmer carry is done switching hands. A weight guideline: men with some training experience — 16 kg, without it — 12 kg; women — 8–12 kg. If you own several, list them in your profile: the app uses a lighter one where the program says "light" (press, snatch, the get-up in the first weeks) and a heavier one where it says "heavy" (deadlifts, carries). You also need a mat.

I have never trained with a kettlebell. Is this for me?

Yes, if you have a general base: 8–10 full push-ups in a row, a one-minute plank, 15–20 squats without getting winded. Kettlebell technique is built from scratch here: the first two weeks are only the deadlift, the swing and the get-up steps; the clean appears in week two, the press out of the clean in week three, the snatch in week five. If the base is not there yet, do Start first — four weeks with no equipment — and come back.

How long is a session?

About half an hour including warm-up and cool-down: strength days and Swing school run 27–38 minutes, metabolic days 21–29, Easy flow about 20, and the benchmark about 18 — short, but the hardest session of the course. Three sessions a week; the final week adds a short technique day before the retest. Before you start, the app shows the estimated duration for your own volume.

I missed a session — what now?

No problem: the path does not reset, just continue from the next node when you can. Do not double up to "catch up" — shifting the week is better. If the break was longer than two weeks, pick "Easier" for the first two sessions back and use a lighter bell on the swings: your grip and palms lose the habit faster than your legs do.

My lower back hurts after swings. Is that normal?

Dull fatigue in the glutes and hamstrings the day after swings is normal. Pain in the lower back itself is not: most often it means you are bending with your back instead of your hips, or letting the bell drop too low toward the knees. Go back to kettlebell deadlifts and swings with a light bell, keep the bell passing high into the groin and squeeze your glutes at the top. Mark "Pain" in the post-workout feedback — the app reduces the load. If the pain is sharp, radiates into a leg or lasts several days, see a doctor.

How does the app adapt the load?

Your starting volume comes from the day-one test. After every session you rate the effort from 1 to 10 and how you felt, and the app nudges the reps up or down for next time. Before each session you can pick Easier, As usual or Harder; the app recommends one based on your recent sessions and how much you have rested. It picks the bell from the ones in your profile using the "light / medium / heavy" labels. In week four the volume drops automatically, and if you flagged lower-back or shoulder issues, heavy swings and presses are swapped for gentler variants.
